Probate basics

A few things worth knowing about probate in California

Every estate is different, and your attorney will guide the legal side. These are the questions executors ask me most about the house.

Probate takes time

Probate runs through the court, and in Los Angeles it often takes many months. Knowing the timeline early helps you plan for the house and its costs.

Bills keep coming

Mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance and utilities usually still need to be paid while the estate is open. Ask your attorney how the estate should handle them.

Selling can happen during probate

Once the court appoints a personal representative, the home can often be sold before probate is finished. How it's done depends on the authority the court grants.

Some sales need court approval

Depending on that authority, a sale may need a court hearing, where other buyers can sometimes make higher offers. I'll plan the listing around what your case requires.

Vacant homes need attention

An empty house can attract break-ins and may affect insurance coverage. Securing it early protects the estate's biggest asset.

Family decisions come first

When several heirs are involved, the best outcome is one everyone understands. Clear numbers make those conversations easier.

Questions

What executors ask me

Do I have to wait until probate is finished to sell the house?

Usually not. Once the court has appointed you and issued your Letters, the home can often be sold during probate. Your attorney will confirm what your authority allows, and I'll plan around it.

Do I need to clean out the house or make repairs first?

No. Many inherited homes sell as-is. Sometimes a light clean-out or a few small fixes are worth it, and sometimes they aren't. I'll give you honest numbers both ways so you can decide.

I live out of state. Can you help?

Yes. I can meet vendors at the property, send video walkthroughs, collect anything important you need, and keep you updated by phone, text or email.

Does the walkthrough cost anything?

No. The walkthrough and my opinion of value are free, with no obligation to list with me.

Do you work with my attorney?

Yes. Your attorney handles the legal process; I handle the house. I'll keep them informed and make sure the sale follows what the court requires.

What if the family disagrees about selling?

It's common. I can walk everyone through the home's value and the options side by side, so the decision is based on the same clear information.
